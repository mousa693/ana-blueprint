# Brand Consistency with AI Across Instagram, Facebook and Threads

Instagram, Facebook and Threads share an owner, an account system and, increasingly, an audience. They don't share a culture. A polished carousel that does well on Instagram can look stiff on Threads. A chatty Threads post can look thin on a Facebook Page.

The goal isn't to post the same thing everywhere. It's to be **recognisably the same brand** everywhere while each post fits its platform. AI makes that easier and riskier at once. It can adapt content quickly, and it can also drift off-voice just as quickly.

This guide covers how to set up for consistency, how to adapt content for each Meta platform, and how to use AI without losing your voice.

## What "brand consistency" actually means on social

Consistency doesn't mean sameness. It means your audience recognises you before they see your handle. Five things carry that recognition:

1. **Voice.** Word choice, sentence rhythm, humour level, formality.
2. **Point of view.** What you believe and argue for.
3. **Visual identity.** Palette, type feel, composition, photo or illustration style.
4. **Themes.** The 3–4 topics you keep coming back to.
5. **Promises.** What you offer and what you never claim.

Format, length, hashtags and posting frequency *should* change between platforms. The five things above shouldn't.

## The platform differences that matter

| | Instagram | Facebook | Threads |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary unit** | Visual (image, carousel, Reel) | Mixed: text + link + image | Text first, conversational |
| **Caption role** | Supports the visual | Often *is* the post | The post itself |
| **Ideal tone shift** | Polished, aspirational | Warmer, more explanatory, community | Casual, quick, opinionated |
| **Typical length** | Short to medium caption | Medium to long | Short |
| **What earns reach** | Saves, shares, watch time | Comments, shares within community | Replies and conversation |
| **Visual weight** | Very high | Medium | Low to medium |

Keep your voice the same across all three columns and change the delivery to fit each one.

## Step 1: Write a one-page voice spec that AI can follow

Long brand books don't change daily output. A one-page voice spec does. Include:

- **Voice in 3–5 pairs:** "Confident, not cocky. Friendly, not cutesy. Expert, not academic."
- **Signature moves:** phrases or structures you use often (a sign-off, a running joke, a recurring format).
- **Never list:** words, emojis, claims and topics that are off-limits.
- **Per-platform dials,** for example:
  - Instagram: short captions, one line break per idea, maximum 1 emoji
  - Facebook: tell the story, end with a question, no hashtags
  - Threads: under 300 characters, first-person, conversational, no hashtag stuffing

The key is putting this spec somewhere the AI reads every time, not in a doc people forget to paste in.

## Step 2: Lock visual identity before generating anything

Visual drift is more noticeable than voice drift. A feed full of AI images in five different styles looks like five different brands.

Before generating visuals:

- **Define the palette** with exact colours, not "warm tones".
- **Choose one primary style:** photography, flat illustration, 3D, collage. Commit for a quarter.
- **Save reference images** of the brand's real products, people and spaces.
- **Set composition rules,** e.g. "subject left third, negative space for text, no busy backgrounds."

## Step 3: Create one core asset, then adapt

The most reliable way to stay consistent across platforms is to start from **one core message** and adapt it, rather than writing three unrelated posts.

**Example: a bakery launching a seasonal pastry**

- **Core message:** "Our pumpkin cardamom bun is back for six weeks only."
- **Instagram:** a close-up carousel (bun, cross-section, baker's hands, shop counter) with the caption: *"It's back. Six weeks only. Pumpkin, cardamom, brown butter glaze. You know what to do."*
- **Facebook:** a photo plus a short story: *"Every October since 2021, someone asks us on day one: 'Is the pumpkin bun back yet?' This year the answer is yes, from Saturday for six weeks. Our head baker Sam tweaked the glaze this year. Come tell us if you can taste the difference."*
- **Threads:** *"Unpopular opinion: pumpkin spice is fine but pumpkin cardamom is better. Back Saturday. Six weeks. Fight us."*

Three formats, one voice, one message. AI is very good at this kind of adaptation **if** it has the voice spec and the core message. Without them, you get three generic posts.

## Step 4: Use AI for adaptation, and humans for the final read

A good workflow for each content batch:

1. Write or generate the **core message** and confirm it's accurate.
2. Ask AI to adapt it for each platform using the per-platform dials.
3. Generate visuals for Instagram first (it carries the most visual weight), then reuse or crop for Facebook.
4. Read all three versions **side by side**. Do they sound like the same brand on a different day?
5. Check every claim: prices, dates, availability.

## Step 5: Schedule Threads, Instagram and Facebook from one place

Publishing to three platforms from three apps is how inconsistency creeps in. A caption gets edited in one place and not the others, or a visual goes out in the wrong aspect ratio.

## Step 6: Audit consistency monthly

Once a month, run a quick consistency audit:

- **Screenshot the last 9 posts on each platform** and put them side by side. Would a stranger know they're the same brand?
- **Read 5 random captions aloud.** Do any sound like a different writer?
- **Check performance by platform.** If Threads posts underperform, is the voice too polished for the platform? If Instagram underperforms, is the visual style drifting?
- **Update the voice spec** with anything you learned.

## Common consistency mistakes with AI

- **Starting every chat from zero.** If your AI tool doesn't remember the brand, you'll get a slightly different voice every time.
- **Mixing image styles.** Choosing a different model or style for every post fragments your grid.
- **Cross-posting identical text.** A 200-word Facebook story pasted into Threads reads as spam.
- **Letting AI add claims.** "Best in town" and "#1 rated" sneak into AI copy. Remove anything you can't prove.
- **No owner.** Someone has to own the voice spec and keep it up to date.
