# How to Turn a Website URL into a Social Media Campaign with AI

Most brands already have everything a social campaign needs. It's sitting on their website: product names and descriptions, the brand's tone, its colours and imagery, who it's for and what it promises.

The trouble is that turning that website into a campaign is usually manual. Someone reads every page, writes a brief, builds a mood board, drafts captions and briefs a designer.

AI can now do the first pass of that work directly from a URL. This guide walks through the process, from link to scheduled posts, and covers what to check at each stage so the output is accurate as well as fast.

## Why start from the website?

A brand's website is usually its most **deliberate** piece of communication. Compared with a chat prompt or a rushed brief, it has:

- **Approved language.** Product names, taglines and claims that have usually been through review.
- **Real visual identity.** Logo, palette, photography style.
- **Structured offering.** Products or services, often with descriptions and prices.
- **Implied audience.** Who the copy is speaking to, and how.

Starting there means AI is working from evidence rather than guesses. It also makes the result easier to check, because every claim can be traced back to a page.

## The URL-to-campaign workflow at a glance

1. **Import:** extract a brand profile from the website (and socials)
2. **Review:** confirm identity, visuals, audience and products
3. **Strategise:** set an objective, channels and a core message
4. **Create:** generate visuals and copy for each calendar entry
5. **Publish:** schedule to Instagram, Facebook and Threads
6. **Learn:** measure and feed results into the next campaign

## Stage 1: Import the brand from its URL

Start by giving the AI the **official** source: the brand's own website, or an official social profile if the website is thin.

In Fuzzbucket you choose **Import my existing brand** and share the link. The assistant then visits the sources, maps the relevant pages and organises what it finds. Processing can carry on in the background while you do other things.

**Tips for a better import:**

- Use the canonical domain, not a landing page built for one campaign.
- If the brand has a strong Instagram but a weak website, offer the Instagram profile as a source.
- Have the brand's real logo and any brand guidelines PDF ready for the review step.

## Stage 2: Review the brand profile

The import gives you a draft **Brand Profile** covering identity, visual guidance, audience information and public presence. It also suggests:

- **Visual directions and palettes**
- **Logo options**
- **Products**, saved as reusable records for later campaigns
- **Brand media**, saved with a record of the source each item came from

You answer clarification questions and accept only the items that accurately represent the brand. This is the most important human step in the whole workflow. Check:

- Is the **audience** specific enough?
- Are the **products** current, and named the way the brand names them?
- Is the **palette** today's, not from a cached old page?
- Did the AI infer any **claims** the website doesn't actually make?

Once confirmed, all of this becomes part of the brand's **Brand Brain**, which is reusable context for every campaign that follows. Building a reusable brand kit from a website takes one review pass rather than a week of design-ops work.

## Stage 3: Turn context into a campaign strategy

With the brand in place, move to strategy before creating anything. A good campaign plan answers:

- **Goal:** what will change if this works? (e.g. "Drive bookings for the new autumn menu")
- **KPI:** how you'll measure it (clicks to booking page, saves, DMs)
- **Channels:** what each platform does in the campaign (Instagram for desire, Facebook for local community, Threads for conversation)
- **Core message:** one sentence the whole campaign repeats
- **Creative direction:** visual mood and format mix

Fuzzbucket's campaign strategy step maps goals, channels and creative direction. Its marketing plans then turn reviewed context into an objective, plan, checklist, calendar entries and next actions.

**Example (fictional brand):**

> **Brand:** Northside Yoga (website: studio pages, class timetable, teacher bios)
> **Goal:** Fill the new 6:30 am weekday class
> **KPI:** Clicks to the booking page; class sign-ups
> **Core message:** "Start before the city does."
> **Lanes:** Instagram (sunrise visuals, teacher intro Reel), Facebook (local community post, early-bird offer the studio has approved), Threads (morning-routine conversation starters)

## Stage 4: Create visuals and copy from the plan

Now you produce assets for each calendar entry. Because the brand context is already loaded, you describe *intent* ("teacher intro for the 6:30 class, calm, sunrise light") rather than writing a long prompt about colours, tone and audience.

In Fuzzbucket's creative studio, a workspace holds chat, a Canvas and creative nodes. You can:

- generate **images** and **short videos** with a choice of models
- generate **audio**, or assemble clips into a longer video
- use **workflow templates** for repeatable formats
- refine one headline, image or caption without regenerating the rest

The homepage example shows the kind of output to expect from a single brief: *"3 assets, 2 captions, 1 launch brief."*

**Quality checklist for this stage:**

- Every product shown is a real product, shown accurately.
- Every offer or price was approved by the brand.
- Captions sound like the website's voice, adjusted for each platform.
- Visuals use the confirmed palette and style.

## Stage 5: Schedule to Instagram, Facebook and Threads

Once assets are approved, connect the brand's Meta accounts and pick targets: a Facebook Page, an Instagram account and a Threads profile. Then for each asset:

1. Review the approved version.
2. Select the destination(s).
3. Schedule or publish now.
4. Check the job status.

Because this happens in the same place as creation, there's no download, re-upload and re-paste step where the wrong file can slip through. (Fuzzbucket publishes through Meta. For LinkedIn, X or TikTok you'd need another tool.)

## Stage 6: Learn and loop

A campaign isn't done when it's published. After the first week, look at:

- **Reach:** did it get seen?
- **Saves:** did people value it?
- **Comments:** did it start conversations?
- **Clicks:** did it drive action toward the KPI?

Fuzzbucket's learning loop uses saved brand memory, marketing plans, suggestions and Meta analytics snapshots to shape the next campaign. In practice, the second campaign for a brand starts smarter than the first.

## Limitations to be honest about

- **Garbage in, garbage out.** A thin or outdated website produces a thin profile. Add sources or fill the gaps manually.
- **AI doesn't know what's off the record.** Upcoming launches, internal politics and legal constraints have to be added by a human.
- **Review is not optional.** Treat AI output as a strong first draft, or "the messy first 80%", in Fuzzbucket's words.
- **Channels.** Publishing here is Meta-only (Facebook, Instagram, Threads).
